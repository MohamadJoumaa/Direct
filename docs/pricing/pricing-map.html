Step 1 · Package · Moto

Method A: start price + price per km

Confirmed. The price grows a little with every km, like a taxi meter, then rounds to the nearest $0.50.

price = start price + (km − km included) × price per km then round to the nearest $0.50 example: $3 + (10 − 3) × $0.25 = $4.75 → client sees $5.00

Why A beat B

Method A · price per km (chosen) Method B · price list by distance group
Two neighboursDistanceMethod AMethod B
Neighbour 114.9 km$5.98$5
Neighbour 215.1 km$6.03$8

With A, similar trips always pay similar prices. With B, 200 metres costs $3 more, so clients feel cheated, argue with drivers, or move the pin to pay less. A is also how the current system already works.

Step 2 · Package · Car

Same method as Moto, with its own numbers

Confirmed. Start price + price per km, rounded to the nearest $0.50. One price per trip, whatever the number of boxes.

example: $6 + (20 − 3) × $0.45 = $13.65 → client sees $13.50
TripMotoCar

Why not "Moto × 2": a car costs more to start any trip (fuel, parking, Beirut traffic), but on long highway trips the gap matters less. Its own numbers let the admin raise the start price without making long trips too expensive.

Boxes: distance only for now

  • Not $5 per box alone: 3 boxes over 2 km would be $15, and 3 boxes over 90 km also $15.
  • Not a client choice: clients always pick the cheaper one, so Direct and the driver always lose.
  • Later, with size: distance price + a fee per extra box. 20 km with 3 boxes = $13.50 + 2 × $2 = $17.50.

Car must cost more than Moto

Wherever both are offered. If the admin's numbers break this, Settings shows a warning (it never blocks or hides anything).

Why plain fields, not a multiplier

Each field means one thing you can explain to a driver: "the first 3 km cost $3, then 25 cents per km". A multiplier like ×1.3 hides what the client actually pays.

Every admin setting so far

  • Range of each option (min and max km)
  • Moto: start price, km included, price per km, rounding
  • Car: start price, km included, price per km, rounding
  • Economy: drop-off fee, company price tables, Direct's share for each way, company on/off and preferred

When Direct picks up, the pickup price depends on how far each company is, so the system compares the full price (pickup + company) and picks the cheapest total. The admin can switch a company off, and mark one as preferred when two cost the same.

Why Direct takes a share

The driver's subscription or percentage only covers the pickup. Without a share, Direct earns nothing on the company part but still handles the order, the support and the risk. It can start at $0 and be raised later.

Economy admin settings

  • Pickup: follows the Moto settings
  • Drop-off fee: default $0
  • Company price tables, one per company
  • Direct's share: one field for each way
  • Company on/off and preferred
  • Direct's drop-off address and hours
  • Pickup from 30 km, drop-off from 20 km, pickup cap $4
  • Economy on/off

Cash on Economy pickup

The client pays the driver the full price at pickup, e.g. $8.50. The driver keeps the pickup part ($3) and owes Direct the rest ($5.50), added to their daily balance like commission. They pay it with Whish or "I already paid". Unpaid after 07:00 the next day, they can't take new jobs.

Later: waiting time for shopping

A separate add-on, picked in blocks before the trip. Example: 15 min $3 · 30 min $6 · 60 min $12. Going over is charged at the same rate, confirmed by the driver.

Findings, now decided

  • Economy pickup vs Moto: at 20 km, pickup + company + share ≈ $8.50 against Moto $7.50. Decided: drop-off from 20 km, Direct pickup from 30 km.
